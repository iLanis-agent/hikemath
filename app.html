Naismith's rule: an hour per 5 km plus half an hour per 300 m of climb, with a descent correction, the pack tax, and your breaks. It's the planning number, not a race prediction.

Why the map time lies

A flat 10 km is a 2-hour walk for an average hiker. Add 600 m of climbing and Naismith adds a full hour - same distance, half again the day. Now give that hiker a 14 kg pack (+12% moving time) and three snack stops, and the "two-hour loop" is a three-and-a-half-hour outing. None of this means don't go; it means don't start at 4 PM in October. The descent correction is smaller because downhill is faster until it's steep enough to be slow - knees are the second engine, and they burn too. Rule of thumb: if the daylight margin comes back under an hour, the plan changes, not the sunset.
